Education for the study commission and for lawmakers comes first

On June 10, 2026 the Rhode Island Senate voted unanimously to create a Special Legislative Commission to Study Blockchain and Cryptocurrency. RIBPI’s main job in 2027 is to help that commission, and the legislators who will act on its findings, work from the public record of what other states have tried.

First Priority
S.2198 Sub A · Resolution 403 ↗

Special Legislative Commission to Study Blockchain and Cryptocurrency

Created June 2026
Sponsors: Sens. DiPalma, Gu, Burke, Urso, Paolino, Zurier Interim report: January 5, 2027 Final report: January 5, 2028 Expires: February 5, 2028

The commission has ten seats. Three go to senators, one to the Secretary of Commerce and one to the Director of the Department of Business Regulation. The other five go to members of the public with backgrounds in academia, banking, federal securities law, blockchain technology and cryptocurrency. The resolution asks the commission to review how other states treat these technologies, hear from experts, and recommend legislation that supports both economic growth and consumer protection. Its final report goes to the General Assembly, the Secretary of Commerce and the General Treasurer. Appointments had not been announced as of November 17, 2026.

What RIBPI will provide

Most of the questions in front of the commission have been tested somewhere else, and the bills, vote counts and results are public. RIBPI will put that record in front of commissioners, legislators and staff in a form they can use.

  • Briefings. Sessions for commissioners, legislators and committee staff on each topic in the commission’s charge, in person or in writing, at their request.
  • State comparisons. For each policy question: which states acted, what the law says, how the vote went, and what happened afterward. The first set is summarized below.
  • Plain-language primers. Bitcoin 101 for legislators and the glossary, kept current through the session.
  • Witness suggestions. Regulators, academics and practitioners from states that have run these programs and can speak to how they worked.
  • Public materials. Everything RIBPI gives the commission will also be posted on this site.

Five questions where other states’ experience can inform the commission

RIBPI is not asking for bills on these in 2027. We will bring the commission the evidence on each.

State oversight of stablecoin issuers has a federal deadline

The federal GENIUS Act takes effect January 18, 2027. A state that wants to supervise smaller issuers under its own law has to file a certification with the U.S. Treasury’s review committee by January 18, 2028. Maryland and Georgia enacted frameworks in 2026. The commission’s final report is due thirteen days before that deadline, so this question is best taken up early.

The kiosk law can do more to stop a scam while it is happening

Rhode Island’s 2025 law sets daily limits and refund rights. In 2026 Vermont, Minnesota, Tennessee and Indiana prohibited kiosks, while North Carolina and New Hampshire kept them and added a 48-hour hold for first-time users. North Carolina also made it a crime to induce a fraudulent kiosk transaction. RIBPI will ask the commission to weigh a hold for first-time users, an offense aimed at the person running the scam, annual public data on complaints and refunds, and a definition tied to in-person cash so that software developers are not swept in.

Holding your own bitcoin and paying with it should stay lawful and unlicensed

South Carolina enacted these protections in 2026 by votes of 38–1 and 110–1, and Pennsylvania’s House passed a similar bill 176–26 in 2024. Rhode Island’s currency transmission law already exempts people who only contribute software or computing power to a network. The open question is how the law treats software that never takes control of a customer’s funds.

Three states authorized bitcoin reserves in 2025, and the results are now visible

New Hampshire, Texas and Arizona passed reserve laws in 2025, and Texas has reported a purchase. Connecticut went the other way and barred the state from holding digital assets. The commission and the General Treasurer should see how each law was written and what has happened since before Rhode Island is asked to decide.

Entity and charter questions from 2026 need a record before they return

The DAO LLC Act (H.7843) and the Economic Growth Blockchain Act (H.7413) were both held for further study in 2026. The commission can ask the Department of Business Regulation what existing trust company law already allows and which entity form other states have found workable.

Three bills we will ask legislators to introduce

Each is narrow and none requires new spending. Two are already law in other states, and the third follows a rule federal law already applies to foreign currency. Bill numbers will be posted here when the bills are filed.

For Introduction

Private Key Protection Act

Returning in 2027
2026 bills: H.7957 (Rep. Casey) and S.2196 (Sen. DiPalma) Models: Wyoming (2023), Utah (2024)

A private key controls every asset, identity and account tied to it. The bill says a court or agency may not compel someone to disclose a private key when a public key, or an order to transfer the asset itself, would give the court what it needs. Courts keep their full authority to order assets produced, accounted for and transferred.

What changes for 2027

The 2026 bills were heard in House Innovation, Internet & Technology on March 5 and in Senate Artificial Intelligence & Emerging Technologies on April 7, and both were held for further study. The 2027 draft adds a definition of “public key” and states plainly that a court can still order the asset itself transferred.

For Introduction

Bitcoin Payments De Minimis Exemption

Revised for 2027
2026 bill: S.2021 (Sen. Appollonio) Heard by Senate Finance June 8, 2026

Under current law, buying a coffee with bitcoin is a taxable sale of property, and the buyer owes a gain or loss calculation on it. The bill removes small everyday purchases from Rhode Island income tax so that bitcoin can be used as money without a recordkeeping penalty.

What changes for 2027

Senate Finance heard S.2021 on June 8, 2026 and held it for further study. The same day the Division of Taxation sent the committee a letter that took no position on the bill and raised drafting and administration concerns. The 2027 draft is written to answer that letter.

  • It covers bitcoin spent on goods and services, and leaves sales of bitcoin for dollars under existing law.
  • It sets a per-transaction threshold modeled on the $200 exclusion that federal law already gives personal purchases made in a foreign currency.
  • It gives administration to the Division of Taxation and measures the exemption from gain the taxpayer has already computed on the federal return.
  • It runs as a pilot long enough for the Division to report on real returns before the General Assembly decides whether to keep it.
  • RIBPI will seek sponsors in both chambers. S.2021 had no House companion.

For Introduction

Unclaimed Digital Assets Act

New for 2027
Amends: R.I. Gen. Laws ch. 33-21.1 (unclaimed property) Models: Virginia, Maine, Utah, Alabama (2026)

When an account sits untouched for years, the company holding it must turn the property over to the General Treasurer, who keeps it for the owner. Rhode Island’s unclaimed property law was written for cash, stock and insurance proceeds. It does not mention digital assets or say what form they should arrive in. Under the bill, abandoned bitcoin would be delivered to the Treasurer as bitcoin and held by a qualified custodian, so that an owner who comes forward gets the asset back.

Why this one is new

Four states enacted this rule in 2026, and each requires the holder to deliver the digital asset in its native form. Virginia’s bill had a Democratic sponsor and passed 96–2 and 40–0. Utah’s passed the Senate 25–0. California and Arizona acted in 2025. The General Treasurer already administers unclaimed property in Rhode Island, so the bill adds a rule to an existing program.
