ResearchRelating to RIBPI’s fiscal analyses, white papers and briefs

Updated November 17, 2026

These are the documents RIBPI gives to committee members, sponsors and reporters. Each one lists its sources. The bill analyses were written for the 2026 session and remain the record for the bills that return in 2027.

Need a briefing? Legislators, staff and journalists can ask Dan for a walkthrough of any paper here. Most take 25 minutes.

Bill analyses, 2026 session

  • S.2021 executive summaryDe minimis exemptionTwo pages for committee members: what the exemption does, at $5,000 a month and $20,000 a year, and what it costs.
  • S.2021 fiscal impact analysisDe minimis exemptionThe $500,000 to $2.5 million annual estimate, with the assumptions, sensitivity tests and JPMorgan Chase Institute data behind it.
  • H.7956 and S.2198 executive summaryStudy commissionA two-page committee primer on the commission and on Wyoming’s 2018 task force.
  • H.7956 and S.2198 fiscal impact analysisStudy commissionEstimates the commission’s operating cost at $25,000 to $40,000 and compares commissions in Wyoming, Virginia, Illinois, Mississippi and Colorado.
  • H.7957 and S.2196 executive summaryPrivate key protectionWhy a private key is a master credential, and how the bill follows Wyoming’s 2023 law.
  • H.7957 and S.2196 fiscal impact analysisPrivate key protectionNo new agency, system or license. Includes a comparison with Wyoming’s law and questions and answers on law enforcement cases.
  • H.7413 executive summaryEconomic Growth Blockchain ActSpecial purpose depository charters and regulatory sandboxes in Wyoming and Nebraska.
  • H.7413 fiscal impact analysisEconomic Growth Blockchain ActCharter and examination fees against startup costs, with a fee schedule modeled on Wyoming’s.
  • H.7843 analysis and recommended amendmentsDAO LLC ActThe drafting gaps RIBPI identified and redline language for each.
  • H.7955 and S.2648 policy briefKiosk definitionsRIBPI’s concerns with the kiosk definition expansion bills.
