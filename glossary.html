GlossaryRelating to the terms used in Bitcoin and digital asset policy

Updated November 17, 2026
22 terms

As used on this site:

  1. “Bitcoin” (BTC) means a decentralized digital currency that lets people transact directly, without an intermediary. It was created in 2009 by the pseudonymous Satoshi Nakamoto and has a fixed supply of 21 million coins. It is often compared to gold: scarce, durable and not controlled by any government, but able to be sent anywhere in minutes.
  2. “Block reward” means the new bitcoin paid to the miner who adds a block. It is cut in half about every four years. The reward has been 3.125 BTC per block since April 2024 and will fall to about 1.56 BTC in 2028.
  3. “Blockchain” means a digital ledger that records transactions across many computers. Each block holds a set of transactions and is cryptographically linked to the one before it, so the chain cannot be altered after the fact. Picture a public notebook that anyone can read, where pages are numbered and sealed and new pages can be added but old ones cannot be changed.
  4. “Cold storage” or “cold wallet” means keeping bitcoin offline, disconnected from the internet, to protect it from hacking, usually on a dedicated hardware device. It is like keeping gold in a safe deposit box and not in your pocket.
  5. “Cryptocurrency” means any digital currency that uses cryptography for security. Bitcoin was the first, and thousands of others have followed. Most differ from Bitcoin in supply, decentralization, governance and security. RIBPI usage: we say “Bitcoin” and “digital assets” and avoid “crypto” as a catch-all, because a fixed-supply decentralized network raises different policy questions than a corporate token or a stablecoin.
  6. “Custodian” or “qualified custodian” means a financial institution that holds and safeguards assets for clients. For bitcoin, qualified custodians such as Coinbase Custody, Fidelity Digital Assets and Anchorage provide institutional security, insurance and regulatory compliance. Policy note: reserve and unclaimed property laws usually require that digital assets be held by a qualified custodian.
  7. “Decentralized” means operating without a central authority or single point of control. Bitcoin is maintained by thousands of independent computers, called nodes, around the world. A bank keeps one set of books. With Bitcoin, everyone in town keeps their own copy.
  8. “Digital asset” means any asset that exists in digital form, including Bitcoin, other cryptocurrencies, stablecoins, NFTs and tokenized securities. Policy note: legislation often uses the term as a catch-all, and some bills narrow it with requirements such as a minimum market capitalization.
  9. “Halving” means the event, about every four years, when the block reward is cut in half, slowing the creation of new bitcoin. The most recent was in April 2024. The reward went from 50 BTC in 2009 to 25 in 2012, 12.5 in 2016, 6.25 in 2020 and 3.125 in 2024.
  10. “Hash rate” means the total computing power being used to mine and process Bitcoin transactions, measured in hashes per second. A higher hash rate means a more secure network.
  11. “Hot wallet” means a wallet connected to the internet. It allows quick transactions and carries more risk than cold storage. It is the cash in your pocket, as opposed to money in a vault.
  12. “Mining” means the process by which transactions are verified and added to the blockchain. Miners run specialized computers to solve a mathematical problem, and the first to solve it adds the next block and receives the block reward. Energy note: 52.4 percent of mining ran on sustainable energy in early 2025, 42.6 percent renewables and 9.8 percent nuclear, according to the Cambridge Centre for Alternative Finance (April 2025).
  13. “Money transmitter” means a business that transfers money or monetary value for the public. Exchanges and some Bitcoin businesses may be classified as money transmitters and need a state license. Policy note: Rhode Island’s 2025 kiosk law requires kiosk operators to be licensed. See Legislation.
  14. “Private key” means the secret cryptographic code that proves ownership of bitcoin and authorizes transactions. Whoever controls the private key controls the bitcoin, so it must be kept secure and never shared. Policy note: the Private Key Protection Act would protect Rhode Islanders from being compelled to disclose one. See the 2027 agenda.
  15. “Proof of work” (PoW) means the mechanism Bitcoin uses to validate transactions and secure the network. Miners must spend computing energy to add a block, which makes attacking the network extremely costly.
  16. “Public key” or “address” means a code derived from the private key that can be shared in order to receive bitcoin. It is like a mailing address: people can send you things with it, but it does not let them into your house.
  17. “Special purpose depository institution” (SPDI) means a bank charter designed for digital asset businesses, which must hold 100 percent liquid reserves. Wyoming and Nebraska offer it. Policy note: H.7413 would have created the charter in Rhode Island. See the 2026 record.
  18. “Stablecoin” means a digital currency designed to hold a stable value, usually one U.S. dollar. USDC and Tether (USDT) are examples. Stablecoins are issued by companies and backed by traditional assets. They are not decentralized the way Bitcoin is.
  19. “Strategic Bitcoin Reserve” (SBR) means a state or national holding of bitcoin kept as a reserve asset, as gold is. Texas, New Hampshire and Arizona passed reserve laws in 2025, and a federal reserve was established by executive order in March 2025. Policy note: these laws usually cap the allocation, set custody requirements and limit eligible assets by market capitalization.
  20. “Tokenization” means representing real-world assets such as real estate, stocks or bonds as digital tokens on a blockchain, which allows fractional ownership and easier transfer. A $10 million building could be divided into 10,000 tokens.
  21. “Unclaimed property” means property a business must turn over to the General Treasurer after the owner has been out of contact for a set period. Policy note: the Unclaimed Digital Assets Act would have abandoned bitcoin delivered as bitcoin. See the 2027 agenda.
  22. “Wallet” means the software or hardware that stores your private keys and lets you send and receive bitcoin. It can be hot (online) or cold (offline). A wallet is closer to a keychain than to a billfold. It holds the keys that prove ownership, not the bitcoin.
