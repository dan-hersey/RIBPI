Rhode Island Bitcoin Policy Initiative

About RIBPIRelating to who we are, what we do and how we are funded

Updated November 17, 2026

SECTION 1. Mission. RIBPI works with Rhode Island lawmakers on Bitcoin and digital asset policy. We brief legislators and their staff, draft bills, write fiscal analyses and testify in committee. The work is nonpartisan and it starts with Rhode Island.

SECTION 2. Founder. Dan Hersey founded RIBPI in 2025. He drafts the bills, writes the fiscal analyses and delivers the testimony. In the 2026 session he testified before House Innovation, Internet & Technology, House Corporations, Senate Artificial Intelligence & Emerging Technologies and Senate Commerce on every bill RIBPI tracked. His background is in technology and policy, and the RIBPI work is unpaid.

SECTION 3. Funding and status. RIBPI is unincorporated and operates as a grassroots research and education initiative. It has no outside funding and no paid lobbyists, and it makes no political contributions. If we incorporate or accept funding, we will say so on this page within seven days.

What we do

SECTION 4. Education and outreach. We sit with committee members and their staff one at a time and answer the questions that come up between hearings. Six bills in the 2026 session can be traced to conversations that started over coffee.

SECTION 5. Drafting and testimony. S.2021, H.7956, H.7957, H.7413 and H.7843 each carried RIBPI material in some form, and Dan testified on every one. We also wrote the policy brief that raised concerns about the kiosk bills, H.7955 and S.2648.

SECTION 6. Research. We publish white papers and one-pagers with a source for every claim. They are on the Research page and are used by bill sponsors, opposing counsel and State House reporters.

History

  1. 2025RIBPI founded. S.0451, a de minimis tax exemption, drafted with its sponsor. S.0373, the first study commission resolution, passed the Senate 38–0 and received no House vote.
  2. January to April 2026Six bills introduced with RIBPI involvement. Dan testified on each. Coverage in RI Current, Bitcoin Magazine, Route Fifty and CoinCentral.
  3. June 8, 2026Senate Finance heard S.2021 and held it for further study.
  4. June 10, 2026The Senate adopted S.2198 Sub A 38–0, creating the Special Legislative Commission to Study Blockchain and Cryptocurrency. Full 2026 record.
  5. November 17, 20262027 agenda published.
  6. January 5, 2027The General Assembly convenes, and the commission’s interim report is due.
  7. In progressBriefing materials and state comparisons for the study commission, and outreach to all 113 Rhode Island lawmakers.
