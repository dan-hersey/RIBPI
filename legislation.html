Education comes first in 2027, with three bills for introduction

The Senate created a study commission on blockchain and cryptocurrency in June 2026. RIBPI’s first job this session is to brief its members and other lawmakers on what other states have tried. Alongside that work we will ask legislators to introduce the Private Key Protection Act, a revised Bitcoin payments de minimis exemption, and a new bill on unclaimed digital assets.

First Priority
S.2198 Sub A · Resolution 403 ↗

Special Legislative Commission to Study Blockchain and Cryptocurrency

Created June 2026
Sponsors: Sens. DiPalma, Gu, Burke, Urso, Paolino, Zurier Interim report: January 5, 2027 Final report: January 5, 2028 Expires: February 5, 2028

The commission has ten seats. Three go to senators, one to the Secretary of Commerce and one to the Director of the Department of Business Regulation. The other five go to members of the public with backgrounds in academia, banking, federal securities law, blockchain technology and cryptocurrency. The resolution asks the commission to review how other states treat these technologies, hear from experts, and recommend legislation that supports both economic growth and consumer protection. Its final report goes to the General Assembly, the Secretary of Commerce and the General Treasurer. Appointments had not been announced as of November 17, 2026.

What RIBPI will provide

Most of the questions in front of the commission have been tested somewhere else, and the bills, vote counts and results are public. RIBPI will put that record in front of commissioners, legislators and staff in a form they can use.

  • Briefings. Sessions for commissioners, legislators and committee staff on each topic in the commission’s charge, in person or in writing, at their request.
  • State comparisons. For each policy question: which states acted, what the law says, how the vote went, and what happened afterward. The first set is summarized below.
  • Plain-language primers. Bitcoin 101 for legislators and the glossary, kept current through the session.
  • Witness suggestions. Regulators, academics and practitioners from states that have run these programs and can speak to how they worked.
  • Public materials. Everything RIBPI gives the commission will also be posted on this site.

Enacted Laws

Legislation signed into law. We support the intent of protecting consumers and have concerns with how the current framework goes about it.

Concerns
S.0016A / H.5121A ↗

Virtual Currency Kiosk Regulation

Enacted June 23, 2025
Signed: June 23, 2025 (P.L. 2025, ch. 113 and 114) Sponsors: Victoria Gu, Julie Casimiro

Creates a licensing and regulatory framework for virtual currency kiosk operators (Bitcoin ATMs) in Rhode Island. Sets daily transaction limits of $2,000 for new customers and $5,000 for existing customers, and requires fee disclosure, on-screen fraud warnings, blockchain analytics, a compliance officer, and refunds for scam victims. The law does not cap fees. No kiosk bill passed in 2026.

Why We Have Concerns

The bill addresses a real problem — kiosk scams that disproportionately harm elderly Rhode Islanders — but its approach is structurally flawed. On-screen disclosure walls mirror failed gift card warning systems ($217M in gift card fraud losses in 2023 despite years of warnings). The entire cost of third-party criminal activity falls on kiosk operators, with cumulative compliance costs exceeding $200,000/year — manageable for national chains but fatal for small RI businesses. Most critically, the bill contains no provisions targeting actual scammers: no enhanced penalties, no law enforcement coordination, no funding for a crypto fraud task force. It regulates the tool while ignoring the criminal.
