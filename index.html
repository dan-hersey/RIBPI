Education comes first

The Senate created a commission to study blockchain and cryptocurrency on June 10, 2026. RIBPI’s first job this session is to brief its members and other lawmakers on what other states have tried, with the bills, the votes and the results. Alongside that work we will ask legislators to introduce three bills. Two are law in other states today, and the third follows a rule that federal law applies to foreign currency.

On the agenda

  • Study commission education first priority
  • Private Key Protection Act returning
  • Bitcoin payments de minimis revised
  • Unclaimed Digital Assets Act new

In the three bills, underlined text is new for 2027 and struck text is what the 2026 bill said.

Private Key Protection Act

A court or agency could not compel a person to hand over a private key when the public key, or an order to transfer the asset, would give it what it needs. The bill returns from 2026 with a definition of “public key.”

Returning in 2027. Not yet filed. Read the section

Bitcoin payments de minimis exemption

Small purchases made with bitcoin would be exempt from state income tax. The 2026 bill covered any transaction up to $5,000 a month. The 2027 draft covers purchases of goods and services under a per-transaction threshold and answers the Division of Taxation’s letter to Senate Finance.

Being redrafted for 2027. Not yet filed. Read the section

Unclaimed Digital Assets Act

This bill is new for 2027. Abandoned bitcoin would reach the General Treasurer as bitcoin, the way abandoned stock arrives as stock. Virginia, Maine, Utah and Alabama enacted this rule in 2026.

New for 2027. In drafting. Read the section
